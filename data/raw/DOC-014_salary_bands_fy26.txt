1. Purpose of this document
===========================

This document lists the pay bands for the financial year FY26. Each band gives the lowest and highest annual fixed pay that the company pays for a job at that level. All amounts are in LPA (lakh rupees per year).

The bands are used when making offers, when reviewing pay and when deciding on promotions. They are reviewed once a year by the head of HR and the head of finance and approved by the Chief Executive.

This document is restricted. It must not be copied, forwarded or discussed outside the small group named in the access list. Individual salaries are never published and are not included here.

2. Handling rules
=================

  - Access is limited to the head of HR and persons named by the head of HR in writing.
  - The document is stored only in the restricted human resources area.
  - Printing is not allowed, except by the head of HR for a meeting, and the copy must be shredded afterwards.
  - Sharing the figures with staff or candidates is not allowed. Offers state a specific amount, not the band.
  - Any suspected leak must be reported at once to the head of HR and the information security manager.
  - Every access to this document is recorded in the audit log.

3. How the bands are built
==========================

The company has six job families: engineering, security, sales, support, human resources and finance. Each family has four levels. A level describes the size of the job, not the person's seniority in years.

Level number | Description                                                
-------------+------------------------------------------------------------
1            | Entry level. Learns the work with guidance                 
2            | Established professional. Works with little guidance       
3            | Senior professional. Leads work and guides others          
4            | Principal or head. Sets direction for an area or a function
(Levels used in all families)

Each band has five reference points: the minimum, the first quartile, the midpoint, the third quartile and the maximum. The midpoint is the market reference for a fully competent person at that level.

17. Career path in the engineering family
=========================================

People in the engineering family move up through four levels. Movement is based on growth of scope and skill, not on time served.

17.1. From Associate Engineer (E1) to Engineer (E2)
---------------------------------------------------

Moving from associate engineer to engineer means a change in scope. The associate engineer works within a range of 6 to 10. The engineer works within 12 to 20. The panel looks for evidence that the person already works at the scope of a engineer and that colleagues rely on them for it.

17.2. From Engineer (E2) to Senior Engineer (E3)
------------------------------------------------

Moving from engineer to senior engineer means a change in scope. The engineer works within a range of 12 to 20. The senior engineer works within 20 to 32. The panel looks for evidence that the person already works at the scope of a senior engineer and that colleagues rely on them for it.

17.3. From Senior Engineer (E3) to Staff Engineer (E4)
------------------------------------------------------

Moving from senior engineer to staff engineer means a change in scope. The senior engineer works within a range of 20 to 32. The staff engineer works within 32 to 48. The panel looks for evidence that the person already works at the scope of a staff engineer and that colleagues rely on them for it.

Lateral moves out of the engineering family are possible and are treated as a new hire into the band that matches the new role, with pay placed by the same rules as an external hire.

26. Using the bands when hiring
===============================

  1. Confirm the level of the role with the hiring manager and the head of HR.
  2. Compare the candidate's experience with the typical experience for the level.
  3. Make the offer within the band. The usual position is between the minimum and the midpoint.
  4. If the candidate needs more than the midpoint, follow the exceptions process.
  5. Record the offer and the reason in the people system.
  6. Never tell the candidate the band. State only the amount offered.

Candidate profile                         | Usual position in band                       
------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------
Meets minimum requirements                | Minimum to first quartile                    
Fully meets requirements                  | First quartile to midpoint                   
Exceeds requirements with strong evidence | Midpoint to third quartile, with approval    
Rare skill with a market premium          | Above third quartile, with executive approval
(Where offers usually fall)

27. Using the bands for promotion
=================================

A promotion moves a person to the next level when they have been working at that level for at least six months and a panel agrees.

  - Evidence of work at the next level over time, not a single project.
  - Support from the manager and one skip-level leader.
  - A vacancy or a clear business need at the higher level.

Pay on promotion moves into the new band. As a guide, the new pay is not less than the minimum of the new band and is normally not more than a fifth above current pay. If current pay is already above the minimum of the new band, the increase is smaller.

Case                           | Guidance                             
-------------------------------+--------------------------------------
Current pay below new minimum  | Move to the new minimum at least     
Current pay within new band    | Increase within the guidance above   
Current pay above new midpoint | Increase only with executive approval
(Pay on promotion)

28. Annual review of the bands
==============================

The bands are reviewed every year before the pay review cycle.

  1. HR collects market data from salary surveys.
  2. Finance confirms the budget for pay.
  3. HR proposes changes to the bands.
  4. The Chief Executive approves the bands.
  5. HR updates this document and the access list.
  6. Managers are told about their individual pay decisions, not about the bands.

Bands are compared with the market at the midpoint. A family or level that is more than ten percent behind the market is flagged for a review within the year.

29. Exceptions
==============

Case                                  | Approver                          
--------------------------------------+-----------------------------------
Offer above the midpoint              | Head of HR                        
Offer above the third quartile        | Head of HR and the department head
Offer above the maximum               | Chief Executive                   
Retention increase above the guide    | Head of HR and the Chief Executive
Temporary allowance outside the bands | Head of HR and the head of finance
(Approvals for exceptions)

Every exception is recorded with the reason, the amount and the approver. Exceptions are reported to the Chief Executive each quarter.

30. Questions and answers
=========================

30.1. Can I tell a manager the band of their team member?
---------------------------------------------------------

Managers may be told the band only on the written permission of the head of HR. They may be told the pay decision for their own team members.

30.2. Does the band include bonuses?
------------------------------------

No. The bands show annual fixed pay only. Variable pay and benefits are described in separate documents.

30.3. What happens when a person reaches the maximum?
-----------------------------------------------------

Pay does not rise above the maximum. Increases above that point are given as one-off payments or by promotion when the person is ready.

30.4. How often are the bands updated?
--------------------------------------

Once a year, for the new financial year.
